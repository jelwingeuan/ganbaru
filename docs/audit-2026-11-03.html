How fast does Ganbaru get a learner to a JLPT pass?

Four specialist agents audited the site: learning science, psychometrics, game design, and accessibility with performance. Three simulated learners blind-solved 20 items each. The two things slowing a pass most are wrong answers that never come back, and a question-ID bug that puts listening items in the vocabulary section of every mock test.

Simulated learner check

This is simulated: three Sonnet agents played N5, N3 and N1 learners. Each solved 20 non-listening items sampled evenly from tests 1 and 2 of its level, without the answer keys. All 60 answers were correct, so the keys look right. They flagged 1 typo, 4 unnatural or ambiguous items, 1 above-level word, 2 near-duplicates, and 14 items as too easy.

Scoring check: blueprint constants match the official JLPT. N1–N3 have three sections of 0–60 with a minimum of 19 each. N4 and N5 combine Language Knowledge and Reading into 0–120 (minimum 38) plus Listening 0–60 (minimum 19). Pass marks are 100, 90, 95, 90 and 80 (content/blueprints.ts:53-171). Scaling is a straight proportion, not IRT equating, and it is labelled as an estimate (lib/scoring.ts:1-24, exam-player.tsx:429).

Three urgent decisions

Decision 1

Fix the broken mock tests now, or regenerate first?

For fixing now

  • It's a small code change: ID scheme, dedupe, blueprint test.
  • Every score shown today is skewed.

Against

  • Until paraphrase and usage items are regenerated, the vocabulary sections run 3 to 8 items short.
  • Regenerating costs agent runs, and you asked to keep usage low.

Recommend: fix the IDs and remove the duplicate slots now. Show the shortened sections honestly ("19 of 24 items"). Regenerate paraphrase and usage in the next content batch.

Decision 2

Build the review scheduler now in local storage, or wait for accounts?

For local-first

  • Takes the 24 h target from 0% to 100% without Supabase keys.
  • Reuses the existing ganbaru:v1:* localStorage pattern.

Against

  • Progress stays on one device.
  • Records need migrating once accounts exist.

Recommend: build it local-first now with versioned, per-item records that can be uploaded once accounts exist.

Decision 3

Next content effort: breadth or depth?

For breadth

  • Coverage is far from 100% (grammar is around 25%).
  • Untested words are the ones learners can't practise at all.

Against

  • No item has been reviewed by a person, and 14 of 60 sampled items are too easy.
  • Adding more unchecked items makes the score estimate less reliable.

Recommend: depth first. Verify the 4,836 drafts (cheapest coverage gain), have a person review a 5% sample, then add grammar items generated from the list with refs.

Build plan: the review loop (decisions 1 and 2)

  1. Make the tests honest (one day). Generate IDs from the full type slug in scripts/ai-unit.ts and scripts/gen-ai-questions.ts. Rename the 58 colliding listening IDs and remove them from section 1. Add a test that fails on duplicate IDs or on a section type that isn't in the blueprint.
  2. Add a scheduler module. lib/srs.ts wraps ts-fsrs. One store, ganbaru:v1:srs, maps an item id to its FSRS card (difficulty, stability, due, lapses, last review). It exposes record(id, correct) and due(level, now). All storage access is wrapped in try/catch.
  3. Record every answer. Drills call record in choose() (drill-player.tsx:48-55). Mock tests record every answered item on finish. A miss maps to Again, which in FSRS means a relearning step of about 10 minutes.
  4. Re-ask within the session. A missed drill question goes back into the queue 3 items later and again at the end, and it has to be answered again.
  5. One click to review. The home page and each level hub get a top button, "Review N due", which leads to /review/[level] and reuses DrillPlayer with the due question list. The header shows the due count.
  6. Fix the copy. The home promise and the card's "Back in…" toast become true, because they now read real due dates.
