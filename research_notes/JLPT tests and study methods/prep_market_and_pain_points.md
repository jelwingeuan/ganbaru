# JLPT Prep Market and Learner Pain Points (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Prices are USD list prices as published at the time of research unless marked otherwise. Older items are marked with their year.

**Source access note:** Reddit (r/LearnJapanese, r/JLPT) could not be searched or fetched; the research tools block reddit.com. Google Play review pages also returned no readable content. Learner voice in these notes therefore comes from the WaniKani and Bunpro community forums, Apple App Store reviews, Hacker News, and review sites (Tofugu, All Language Resources, Mezzoguild). The report writer should not present these findings as a Reddit sample.

## 1. Which JLPT prep tools lead in 2025–2026, and what do they offer?

### Takeaway
No single tool covers the whole JLPT. Some tools are SRS-first: WaniKani for kanji, Bunpro for grammar and vocab, Anki, jpdb, Renshuu and MaruMori. They have strong progress tracking but no mock tests, or only added them recently. Others are exam-first: Migii, Todaii, Kanshudo's JLPT Resource Center, and a crop of small AI mock-exam apps. These have mock tests but weaker learning pedagogy and frequent quality complaints. Bunpro (September 2025) and Kanshudo (December 2025) are the main SRS players that recently moved into timed mock tests. Demand has surged: about 1.94M applicants in 2025, and visas now require N2 or B2 for some categories.

### Inferences
- The market splits into two camps:
  - **Learning engines** (Bunpro, WaniKani, Anki, jpdb, Renshuu, MaruMori) are good at retention and tracking, but weak at exam realism.
  - **Exam drillers** (Migii, Todaii, AI mock apps) have mock tests but face complaints about content accuracy and pedagogy.
- Bunpro (Sep 2025) and Kanshudo (Dec 2025) are converging toward the planned app's combined scope. Kanshudo is the closest analog because it combines a dictionary, flashcards, JLPT lists, timed tests and a readiness assessment.
- No major SRS-first tool was found offering FSRS for grammar. Bunpro users are asking for it. Anki has FSRS but no JLPT structure.
- Seat shortages and visa-driven demand (N2 for work, B1 for SSW(ii) and permanent residency from 2027) raise the stakes of each sitting. That increases the value of honest readiness estimates and study plans tied to the exam date.
- Pricing anchors:
  - Monthly: $5 (Bunpro) to $9 (WaniKani, MaruMori, Nihongo Pass, Satori).
  - Annual: about $44–$96.
  - Lifetime: $110–$350.
  - Generous free tiers (Renshuu, jpdb, JLPT Sensei) set the expectation that core study is free.

## 2. What do learners complain about most?

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints fall into six groups:
1. SRS review pile-ups and rigid scheduling.
2. The gap between "knowing" items in SRS and performing under exam conditions, especially listening and finishing reading in time.
3. Mock tests that are easier or less realistic than the real exam.
4. Paywall and subscription terms.
5. Content errors, including suspected AI-generated errors, in exam-drill apps.
6. Fuzzy, unofficial JLPT level lists.

An exam-level pain also matters: seats are scarce and a failure is costly.

### Cited Findings

#### SRS review pile-ups and rigid scheduling
- Bunpro users trace pile-ups to adding too many lessons. One user calculated that 40 lessons/day leads to about 500–800 daily reviews. Ghost reviews and hourly intervals add to the load. — [Bunpro forum: "Too many reviews?"](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/too-many-reviews-getting-out-of-hand/119297); [search summary of related threads](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/frustrated-with-the-srs-implementation-bunpro-feedback/134152)
- June 2025, Bunpro: a user reports review accuracy often below 70% and asks for FSRS. Another says Bunpro "relies on a fixed interval system", and a third says late-stage frequency is "too low, leading to poor retention". FSRS is said to be "coming" with no date, which is secondhand and not from staff. — [Bunpro forum, 2025-06-15](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/frustrated-with-the-srs-implementation-bunpro-feedback/134152)
- Other Bunpro threads: "Why do I suddenly got bombarded with 80+ reviews?" and "Overloaded with reviews…" — [thread](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/why-do-i-suddenly-got-bombarded-with-80-reviews/147751); [thread](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/overloaded-with-reviews-and-not-sure-how-to-proceed/57584)
- WaniKani: after a break you can be "swarmed with hundreds of kanji" (reader comment). — [Mezzoguild](https://www.mezzoguild.com/wanikani-review)

#### Knowing words vs. passing the exam
- A WaniKani forum user studied about 2 hours/day for 2 years with Genki and WaniKani and missed N5 by a couple of points. They then got nearly the same result on N4. Replies:
  - "Study the test": the advice that for N5–N3 you must learn the test format itself.
  - Get non-multiple-choice practice.
  - Diagnose knowledge vs. application.
  — [WK forum, Jan 2023](https://community.wanikani.com/t/another-jlpt-failure-any-advice/60271)
- HN discussion (Apr 2026) of the N2 visa rule: the JLPT tests only reading and listening, and people can pass N2 without holding a conversation. — [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47796197) (comments by timr, wk_end, maxgashkov)

#### Reading time pressure
- After the December 2025 JLPT: one N1 taker "barely finished" this time. Another could not finish the reading and reached the second-to-last passage with too little time. — [WK forum "JLPT 2025!" p.12, Dec 7–8, 2025](https://community.wanikani.com/t/jlpt-2025/69343?page=12)
- Older advice threads (2021) focus on time allocation: skip hard items and scan the questions first. — [WK forum](https://community.wanikani.com/t/jlpt-how-much-time-should-one-spend-on-each-section/54050)

#### Listening is the most feared section
- Listening is often named the hardest section. Poor test-center audio made it worse in Dec 2025, including a CD player held to a microphone. Instructions in the local language (Spanish), which differed from practice materials, also threw a candidate off. — [WK forum p.12](https://community.wanikani.com/t/jlpt-2025/69343?page=12); [WK forum p.11](https://community.wanikani.com/t/jlpt-2025/69343?page=11)
- Bunpro mock users (Oct 2025):
  - Listening felt harder than the real test, because you cannot read the answer choices ahead.
  - There is no pause time built in after questions.
  - Narrow listening passes are common.
  — [Bunpro JLPT tests thread p.9](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/bunpro-jlpt-tests-new-feature-sep-20th-2025/149777?page=9)

#### Unrealistic mock tests

  - The timer is a soft limit and keeps running after you leave.
  - Listening replays are unlimited.
  — [Bunpro thread p.9](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/bunpro-jlpt-tests-new-feature-sep-20th-2025/149777?page=9)

#### Exam access

### Inferences
- The most product-relevant pain is transfer. Learners can clear SRS queues but still fail timed reading and single-play listening. An app that measures performance under exam conditions, rather than SRS "known" counts, addresses this directly.
- Review pile-ups are partly a UX problem: lesson pacing, no caps, and catch-up after breaks. They are partly a scheduler problem: fixed intervals. FSRS with a daily review budget and an over-capacity warning would answer both.
- Accuracy is a trust differentiator. Exam-drill apps that use AI-generated questions draw "misleading answers" complaints. Human-reviewed content, and transparency about any AI use, is a credible position.
- Level lists should be labeled honestly, for example "estimated level, unofficial, source X", because learners notice the inconsistencies.

## 3. Which features do learners want that no tool does well?

### Takeaway
The clearest unmet needs:
- Mock tests that behave like the real exam: a hard timer, single-play listening with read-ahead and answer gaps, and correct section structure and instructions.
- Section-level diagnosis that maps to the official scaled-score pass rules.
- Listening and reading-speed training rather than more flashcards.
- A plan that works backward from the exam date and keeps the SRS load sustainable.

No tool surveyed does all of these. Most do one.

### Cited Findings
- **Exam realism requests (Bunpro mocks, Oct 2025):**
  - Built-in listening pause time; staff said they plan to fix it.
  - A timer that resumes correctly or can be reset.
  - Checking answers mid-test.
  - Read-ahead of listening choices, the gap that made practice listening harder than the real exam.
  — [Bunpro thread p.9](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/bunpro-jlpt-tests-new-feature-sep-20th-2025/149777?page=9)
- **Realism as a niche selling point:** At least one new web product sells realism explicitly: timer, single-play listening, no pausing, instant section breakdown. — [PassJapanese](https://passjapanese.com/en/exam-modes) (via search summary; page returned 403)
- **Official section timings** that a realistic mock must reproduce:

  | Level | Vocab/grammar and reading | Listening |
  |---|---|---|
  | N1 | 110 min (combined) | 55 min |
  | N2 | 105 min (combined) | 50 min |
  | N3 | 30 + 70 min | 40 min |
  | N4 | 25 + 55 min | 35 min |
  | N5 | 20 + 40 min | 30 min |

  Listening times vary slightly with the recording. N4 and N5 times changed in Dec 2020, and N1 listening in Dec 2022. — [jlpt.jp test sections](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html)
- **Listening:** Forum advice says to practice with material you can read and listen to at the same time, at or slightly above your level, and to shadow it. Few apps structure this around the JLPT. — [WK forum search summary](https://community.wanikani.com/t/what-is-it-with-listening-to-japanese-speech/66210)
- **Studying the test format:** Learners say you must learn the question types at N5–N3. Bunpro staff advise learning the formats because they are consistent. — [WK forum 2023](https://community.wanikani.com/t/another-jlpt-failure-any-advice/60271); [Bunpro thread p.9](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/bunpro-jlpt-tests-new-feature-sep-20th-2025/149777?page=9)
- **Practice by question type:** Kanshudo offers this (14 types at N5). Most others do not. — [Kanshudo blog](https://www.kanshudo.com/blog/jlpt)
- **Study plans:**
  - Migii advertises a "personalized roadmap" based on strengths and weaknesses. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1463267540)
  - The AI mock app advertises an "AI Study Plan" based on strengths and weaknesses. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6738001895)
  - Kanshudo gives a progress assessment toward the next exam. — [Kanshudo blog](https://www.kanshudo.com/blog/jlpt)
  - None of the listings explicitly describes planning backward from the exam date, such as daily new-item quotas computed from the date and the projected review load.
- **FSRS for grammar and vocab with tunable retention** is requested by Bunpro users. — [Bunpro forum, Jun 2025](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/frustrated-with-the-srs-implementation-bunpro-feedback/134152)
- **Load planning:** Bunpro users compute and complain about the load from lesson pace, which suggests they want projections before committing. — [Bunpro forum](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/too-many-reviews-getting-out-of-hand/119297)
- **Reading practice:** Graded reading exists: Bunpro has 120+ passages, plus Satori Reader and Todaii news. No tool found offers timed reading-speed training calibrated to JLPT passage lengths and time budgets. — [bunpro.jp/pricing](https://bunpro.jp/pricing); [Satori](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1382950847); [Todaii review](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/todai/)
- **Instruction language:** A Dec 2025 candidate was thrown by spoken instructions in the local language (Spanish), having practiced only with English. — [WK forum p.12](https://community.wanikani.com/t/jlpt-2025/69343?page=12)
- **Speaking** is absent from the JLPT and a frequent complaint, but it is outside the exam's scope. — [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47796197)

### Inferences
- The positioning gap for an original app: one place that links dictionary lookup, FSRS cards ("red sheet" recall), grammar and timed mocks into a single readiness model, with the plan computed from the exam date.
- Highest-value differentiators, based on learner evidence:
  1. Strict exam mode: a hard timer, single-play listening with official-style pauses and read-ahead, and the real section order and timings.
  2. Section-level readiness against the official sectional minimums.
  3. A daily plan that caps the review budget and back-schedules new items to the exam date.
  4. Timed reading drills sized to real passage lengths.
  5. Listening drills with transcripts and shadowing.
- Gamification should reward exam-realistic practice, such as finishing a timed reading set or doing single-play listening, not just streaks. Renshuu and MaruMori already gamify SRS heavily.

### Gaps
- No direct learner survey of feature requests was found. Reddit was blocked, and no survey data turned up.
- Whether any app truly plans backward from the exam date, beyond marketing language, could not be verified without hands-on use.
- No published data shows that any mock test's scores correlate with real JLPT results.

## 4. Do any apps predict pass probability or JLPT score, and how?

### Takeaway
A few apps advertise prediction: Nihongo Pass (pass probability) and Migii (a paid "Prediction Test"). Neither publishes its method or any validation. Most mock tools report raw section scores against the cutoffs. The official JLPT uses Item Response Theory scaled scores that cannot be derived exactly from raw counts, so any predictor is an approximation. The honest approach is a range with section-level minimum checks.

### Cited Findings
- **Official scoring:**
  - The JLPT uses scaled scores based on Item Response Theory and "does not total allocated points" of correct answers. — [JLPT FAQ §5](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)
  - The scaled score depends on each examinee's answer pattern. A candidate with more correct answers can receive a relatively modest scaled score. — [JLPT FAQ §5](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)
- **Pass rules (in effect since 2010):** Each level is scored 0–180. Candidates must meet the overall pass mark and every sectional minimum. — [jlpt.jp scoring](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html)

  | Level | Overall pass mark | Sectional minimums |
  |---|---|---|
  | N1 | 100 | 19/60 in each of 3 sections |
  | N2 | 90 | 19/60 in each of 3 sections |
  | N3 | 95 | 19/60 in each of 3 sections |
  | N4 | 90 | 38/120 (language and reading), 19/60 (listening) |
  | N5 | 80 | 38/120 (language and reading), 19/60 (listening) |
- **Past papers:** Exact past questions are not published. The official Practice Workbooks (2012, and Vol. 2 in 2018) contain selected past items, and sample questions with audio are available. — [JLPT FAQ §4](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)
- **Nihongo Pass:**
  - Claims its algorithm "predicts your actual JLPT scores based on your retention rate".
  - Says "AI analyzes every answer to predict your pass chance in real time".
  - Shows a pass-probability percentage (78% in the example), section readiness and section cutoffs.
  - No formula, training data or accuracy validation is published. Prediction is a $9.99/month Pro feature.
  — [nihongopass.com](https://nihongopass.com/)
- **Migii "JLPT Prediction Test" (T7/2026):** Paid and bought in the app. The page gives no price, format or scoring method. — [jlpt.migii.net](https://jlpt.migii.net/en/prediction-test)
- **Kanshudo:** Gives "an overall assessment of your progress towards the next exam". No numeric score or pass prediction is described. — [Kanshudo blog](https://www.kanshudo.com/blog/jlpt)
- **GyanMirai JLPT Score Calculator:** Converts raw section scores into an approximate scaled score. It states it tends to under-predict by 3–5 points and calls itself a planning tool, not a prediction tool. This is the most transparent tool found. — [GyanMirai](https://www.gyanmirai.com/tools/jlpt-score-calculator) (via search summary)
- **Best-Calculators JLPT calculator:** Checks known scaled scores against the two-part pass rule. It does not forecast. — [best-calculators.com](https://best-calculators.com/education-academic/jlpt-score/)
- **JLPTMatome:** Its simulation decides pass or fail from section minimums, not a statistical model. — [JLPTMatome](https://www.jlptmatome.com/jlpt-simulation) (via search summary)
- **MyJLPT:** Tracks score progress across past-paper-style tests. No prediction claim was found. — [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/myjlpt/1643274465)
- **Bunpro mocks:** Report raw section scores, such as "24 out of 26". Users did not see or request a pass estimate in the thread reviewed. — [Bunpro thread p.9](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/bunpro-jlpt-tests-new-feature-sep-20th-2025/149777?page=9)
- **AI Mock Exam app:** Offers "progress insights" but no score forecasting. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6738001895)

### Inferences
- Because scoring is IRT-based and item difficulties are unpublished, a credible predictor should:
  1. Estimate each section's scaled score as a range, calibrated from mock performance.
  2. Apply both the overall mark and the sectional minimum. Learners often fail on a single section, such as listening, while the total looks fine.
  3. Show its uncertainty and method openly.
- That transparency would stand apart from Nihongo Pass's and Migii's opaque claims.
- Nihongo Pass bases prediction on SRS retention. Given the transfer gap in Q2, a predictor driven mainly by retention is likely to overestimate readiness. Timed mock results should carry more weight than SRS retention.
- A reasonable framing for the planned app: "readiness estimate," not "pass guarantee." Recalibrate it whenever users share real score reports.

### Gaps
- No app was found that publishes validation data showing predicted vs. actual JLPT results.
- Migii's prediction method and price are unknown.
- The JLPT's separate scaled-score PDF, referenced in the FAQ, was not read. It may describe the score distribution in more detail.
- Official "reference information" (A/B/C ratings for vocabulary, grammar and reading subskills on N1–N3 score reports) was not covered on the pages fetched.
