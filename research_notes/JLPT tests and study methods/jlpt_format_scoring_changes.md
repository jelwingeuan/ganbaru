# JLPT (Japanese-Language Proficiency Test) N5–N1: format, scoring, recent changes and statistics (as of 26 October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Primary sources are jlpt.jp pages and PDFs, which the Japan Foundation and JEES jointly run. Secondary sources are used only where noted and are flagged as such. Information older than 2025 is labelled with its year. These notes describe structure only and reproduce no actual test questions.

Terminology used below. 大問 (mondai) is a question type or block. 小問 is an individual question. Official English names follow the jlpt.jp English pages. Some official English names have two variants: "Comprehension of key points" is also "Point comprehension", "Comprehension of general outline" is also "Summary comprehension", and "Verbal expressions" is also "Utterance expressions".

## Key Question 1: Test sections, times and 問題 (mondai) types for each level N5–N1

### Takeaway
N1 and N2 have two test sections: a combined Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar)・Reading section (110 and 105 min) and Listening (55 and 50 min). N3, N4 and N5 have three: Vocabulary (30, 25 and 20 min), Grammar・Reading (70, 55 and 40 min) and Listening (40, 35 and 30 min). The official question types (大問) and their approximate counts are tabulated below. The only official changes to time or item counts since the 2010 redesign are N4/N5 from December 2020 and N1 Listening from December 2022.

#### C. What each question type tests (official purposes, paraphrased in plain English)
Paraphrased from the official ねらい (purpose) column in the per-level PDFs: [n1_revised.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/guideline/pdf/n1_revised.pdf), [n2.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/guideline/pdf/n2.pdf), [n3.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/guideline/pdf/n3.pdf), [n4_revised.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/guideline/pdf/n4_revised.pdf), [n5_revised.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/guideline/pdf/n5_revised.pdf), and the English equivalents [n1_e.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n1_e.pdf), [n2_e.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n2_e.pdf), [n3_e.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n3_e.pdf), [n4_e.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n4_e.pdf), [n5_e.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n5_e.pdf).

- **漢字読み Kanji reading.** Tests the reading of a word written in kanji (choose the correct kana reading).
- **表記 Orthography.** Tests how a word written in hiragana is written in kanji. At N5 the target can be kanji or katakana.
- **語形成 Word formation (N2 only).** Tests knowledge of derived and compound words, such as prefixes, suffixes and compounds.
- **文脈規定 Contextually-defined expressions.** Tests which word fits, in meaning, a slot in a sentence.
- **言い換え類義 Paraphrases.** Tests choosing the word or expression closest in meaning to an underlined word or expression.
- **用法 Usage.** Tests how a given word is used in a sentence (pick the sentence where it is used correctly).
- **文の文法1（文法形式の判断） Sentential grammar 1.** Tests choosing the grammar form that fits the content of a sentence.
- **文の文法2（文の組み立て） Sentential grammar 2.** Tests assembling a syntactically correct, meaningful sentence by ordering the given pieces.
- **文章の文法 Text grammar.** Tests choosing what fits the flow of a passage (cloze within a longer text).
- **内容理解（短文） Comprehension, short passages.** Tests reading a short text and understanding its content. Lengths: N1 and N2 about 200 characters (explanations or instructions, including life and work topics); N3 about 150–200; N4 about 100–200 (easy text on study, life or work); N5 about 80.
- **内容理解（中文） Comprehension, mid-size passages.** Tests reading a mid-length text. N1: about 500 characters (reviews, commentary, essays), for causal relations and reasons. N2: about 500 characters of relatively easy reviews, commentary or essays, for causes, reasons, outline and the author's ideas. N3: about 350 characters (commentary, essays), for key words and causal relations. N4: about 450 characters. N5: about 250 characters, on everyday topics.
- **内容理解（長文） Comprehension, long passages (N1 and N3 only).** N1: about 1,000 characters (commentary, essays, novels), for the outline and the author's ideas. N3: about 550 characters (commentary, essays, letters), for the summary and logical development.
- **統合理解 Integrated comprehension, reading (N1 and N2).** Tests comparing and integrating several texts, about 600 characters in total. At N2 the texts are relatively easy.
- **主張理解（長文） Thematic comprehension, long passages (N1 and N2).** Tests grasping the overall argument or opinion. N1: about 1,000 characters of abstract, logical editorials or critiques. N2: about 900 characters of critiques with relatively clear logic.
- **情報検索 Information retrieval.** Tests finding the needed information in practical material. N1 and N2: ads, brochures, magazines and business documents, about 700 characters. N3: ads and brochures, about 600 characters. N4: guides and notices, about 400 characters. N5: notices, about 250 characters.
- **課題理解 Task-based comprehension, listening.** Tests listening to a coherent text, extracting what is needed to solve a concrete task, and deciding what to do next.
- **ポイント理解 Comprehension of key points, listening.** Tests listening with a focus given in advance and narrowing in on the key points.
- **概要理解 Comprehension of general outline, listening (N1–N3).** Tests understanding the speaker's intention or opinion from the text as a whole.
- **発話表現 Verbal expressions, listening (N3–N5).** Tests choosing the appropriate utterance for a situation while looking at an illustration.
- **即時応答 Quick response, listening.** Tests choosing an appropriate reply to a short utterance, such as a question.
- **統合理解 Integrated comprehension, listening (N1 and N2).** Tests comparing and integrating several pieces of information while listening to a relatively long text.

### Inferences
- Total approximate questions under the current official figures, as Language Knowledge/Reading plus Listening: **N1 71 + 30 = 101; N2 75 + 32 = 107; N3 74 + 28 = 102; N4 57 + 28 = 85; N5 43 + 24 = 67.** These are sums of the official "approximate" counts. Real papers can differ by a few items.
- For a mock-test generator, store the counts as configurable ranges, not constants, since the JLPT says they "may vary slightly from session to session". Reading items are per question, not per passage. For example, N1 中文 has 9 questions spread over several passages.
- The N1 language-knowledge counts come from a 2009 document. The 2022 notice changed only Listening, so these are still the latest official figures. Commercial prep books sometimes report slightly different counts from recent real papers (for example, 文章の文法 with 4 instead of 5). I did not verify these.
- The 2022 notice's "before" figures for N1 Listening (6/7/6/13/3 = 35) differ from the 2009 guidebook (6/7/6/14/4 = 37). This suggests an unannounced or earlier adjustment between 2009 and 2022. The current figure is 30.

### Gaps
- I found no official current (post-2010) item counts for N2 and N3 other than the 2009 guidebook. jlpt.jp has published no change notice for N2 or N3, so the guidebook figures are assumed current.
- I found no official figure for how many passages each reading mondai uses, nor for listening audio length per item.
- Official vocabulary, kanji and grammar lists: not researched here (the JLPT has not published official lists since 2010, but I did not verify this in this session).

## Key Question 2: How scoring works (scaled scores 0–180, scoring sections, overall and sectional pass marks, IRT and equating)

### Takeaway
Every level is scored on a 0–180 scaled score. N1–N3 report three sections of 0–60 each (Language Knowledge, Reading, Listening). N4 and N5 report Language Knowledge・Reading as 0–120 and Listening as 0–60. To pass, a candidate must reach the overall pass mark (N1 100, N2 90, N3 95, N4 90, N5 80) **and** every sectional minimum (19/60, or 38/120 for the N4/N5 combined section). Scores are IRT-based scaled scores equated across sessions, not raw counts of correct answers.

### Cited Findings
- Scoring sections and ranges. **N1, N2, N3:** Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar) 0–60, Reading 0–60, Listening 0–60, total 0–180. **N4, N5:** Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar)・Reading 0–120, Listening 0–60, total 0–180. — [JLPT – Scoring Sections / Pass or Fail](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html); [Guidebook Executive Summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/reference/pdf/guidebook_s_e.pdf)
- Pass marks (overall / sectional):

| Level | Overall pass mark | Sectional pass marks |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | 100 / 180 | 19 / 60 in each of Language Knowledge, Reading, Listening |
| N2 | 90 / 180 | 19 / 60 in each of the three |
| N3 | 95 / 180 | 19 / 60 in each of the three |
| N4 | 90 / 180 | 38 / 120 (Language Knowledge・Reading); 19 / 60 (Listening) |
| N5 | 80 / 180 | 38 / 120 (Language Knowledge・Reading); 19 / 60 (Listening) |

  These standards have applied since July 2010 (December 2010 for N4 and N5). — [JLPT – Pass or Fail](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html)
- Falling below any sectional pass mark means failing, however high the total. Missing any test section means failing; the Score Report then shows no section scores (asterisks, for example "＊＊/60"). — [JLPT – Pass or Fail](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html)
- Purpose of sectional minimums: to "assess the comprehensive proficiency". — [Guidebook Executive Summary p.5](https://www.jlpt.jp/reference/pdf/guidebook_s_e.pdf)
- How test sections map to scoring sections. For N1–N3 the scoring sections are Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar), Reading and Listening. At N3, grammar items sit in the Grammar・Reading *test* section but count towards the Language Knowledge *scoring* section. For N4 and N5, the Vocabulary and Grammar・Reading test sections combine into one 0–120 scoring section. — [Guidebook Executive Summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/reference/pdf/guidebook_s_e.pdf); [Characteristics of the New JLPT (2011)](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/reference/pdf/guide2011_e_02.pdf). (A model summary of [results.html](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html) found that page's N3 mapping table ambiguous. The reading above follows the guidebook's scoring-section definitions.)
- IRT and scaled scores. "The JLPT uses a scaled score system based on 'Item Response Theory' and does not total allocated points" of correctly answered questions. The scaled score is "determined based on each examinee's individual answering pattern", so "it is possible for an examinee with a high number of correct answers to receive a relatively modest scaled score." — [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)
- Equating. "scores for the new JLPT will be equated from session to session within the same level". The aim is to remove the effect of small difficulty differences between sessions. — [Guidebook Executive Summary p.3 (2009)](https://www.jlpt.jp/reference/pdf/guidebook_s_e.pdf). "The same scale is always used for the same-level test. Therefore, regardless of difficulty of tests at different times, examinees with the same proficiency have the same score." — [Characteristics of the New JLPT (2011)](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/reference/pdf/guide2011_e_02.pdf)
- Old test vs new test. Pass lines for the current test were statistically designed to match the old (pre-2010) test, but scores "cannot be compared". — [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html)
- Score Report contents. It shows pass or fail, section scaled scores, the total, and "reference information". The reference information is a band for each component, based on the percentage of questions answered correctly: A = 67% or more, B = 34–66%, C = less than 34%. For N1–N3 it covers "Vocabulary" and "Grammar" (inside the Language Knowledge section); for N4 and N5 it covers "Vocabulary", "Grammar" and "Reading". The reference information "is not used to determine pass or fail". — [Characteristics of the New JLPT (2011)](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/reference/pdf/guide2011_e_02.pdf); [Guidebook Executive Summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/reference/pdf/guidebook_s_e.pdf)
- All examinees receive a Score Report, and those who pass also receive a Certificate of Proficiency. — [JLPT – results.html](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html)
- CEFR reference level (new from December 2025). For passers only, the total score is mapped to a CEFR level as reference information (see Key Question 4). — [JLPT – CEFR reference](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/cefr_reference.html)

### Inferences
- A mock-test app cannot reproduce official scaled scores exactly, because the JLPT does not publish item parameters. A reasonable approach is a linear raw→scaled approximation for each scoring section (0–60, or 0–120 for N4/N5), with the official sectional and overall pass rules applied on top. Label the result clearly as an estimate. The FAQ's own statement that a high raw score can map to a modest scaled score supports this caveat.
- The A/B/C reference bands are raw-percentage based and officially defined, so an app can reproduce them exactly.

### Gaps
- I found no official public description of the IRT model (1PL, 2PL or 3PL), the anchor-item design, or the raw-to-scaled conversion tables.
- Percentile rank: I found no current official confirmation of whether score reports or certificates show a percentile rank. Official "scaled score cumulative distribution" PDFs are published for each session (linked from the data pages), but I did not read them.

## Key Question 4: Recent and announced changes (CBT, registration, new countries/sites, can-do/CEFR, format, 2026–2027 plans), separating confirmed facts from rumours

### Takeaway
**Confirmed (official):** CEFR reference levels have been shown on results for passers since December 2025. Overseas candidates have been banned from carrying phones and electronic devices since a March 2025 notice. Copies of score certificates became paid from April 2025. A test-accommodations page went up in February 2026. New test countries and cities were added for December 2025 and July 2026. There has been no format change since December 2022. **No official announcement of a CBT (computer-based) JLPT** was found. **Reported but not JLPT policy:** early registration closures in Japan in 2026 because of venue shortages; a MEXT (education ministry) request that schools consider alternative tests; proposals for more JLPT sittings. These are documented in the press, not in JLPT format announcements.

**Confirmed changes (official sources)**
- **Format changes since 2010:** (1) N4/N5 times and item counts reduced from the December 2020 test (see Key Question 1). ### Inferences
- Pass rates are fairly stable at about 28–38% for N1–N4 and about 50% for N5. A mock app could show these as context ("about 1 in 3 pass N1–N4").
